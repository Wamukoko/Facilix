The biggest opportunity is not to build another generic CMMS. **openMAINT is broad and highly configurable but can be difficult to deploy and operate; UpKeep is easy and mobile-first but can become restrictive, expensive, and shallow for complex facility operations.** A new app could win by combining openMAINT’s depth with UpKeep’s usability while solving frontline execution, offline work, integrations, contractor coordination, and reliability analytics.

## Highest-value wedge

A particularly strong product position would be:

> **An offline-first facility maintenance operating system that turns every job into structured reliability data and automatically coordinates staff, contractors, inventory, compliance, and building systems.**

This addresses the space between the two products:

- From **openMAINT**, borrow asset hierarchy, location modeling, GIS/BIM relationships, preventive/corrective workflows, costs, inventory, and configurable processes. [openmaint](https://www.openmaint.org/en/sitemap)
- From **UpKeep**, borrow rapid deployment, simple mobile work orders, QR-based access, checklists, notifications, and approachable field workflows. [upkeep](https://upkeep.com/demo/)
- Add what both leave comparatively exposed: reliable offline use, failure intelligence, deep procurement, contractor collaboration, open integrations, and facility-wide context.

## Features worth building first

1. **One-minute work request**
   - QR/NFC scan, photo/video, voice description, location auto-detection, urgency, and requester contact.
   - AI suggests asset, category, priority, safety risk, and likely procedure—but allows human approval.

2. **Technician execution mode**
   - Offline checklist, step-by-step instructions, diagrams, manuals, safety controls, parts list, time capture, and signature.
   - No need to navigate a large CMMS database.

3. **Intelligent closeout**
   - Require only the minimum useful fields.
   - Convert voice notes into structured failure, cause, remedy, and recommendation codes.
   - Detect vague entries such as “fixed” or “other.”

4. **Failure and PM intelligence**
   - Detect repeat failures by asset, room, component, contractor, and failure mode.
   - Show whether PM actually reduces breakdowns.
   - Recommend changes to frequency, checklist, spare parts, or replacement strategy.

5. **Contractor workspace**
   - Issue jobs, manage quotes, verify compliance documents, capture evidence, approve work, and calculate SLA and first-time-fix performance.

6. **Open operational platform**
   - API, webhooks, import/export, BMS/IoT integration, ERP/accounting connectors, and public data dictionary.
   - Avoid forcing customers to buy a separate analytics or IoT product for basic operational insight.

## What to validate before building

These are promising gaps, not proof that every customer experiences them. UpKeep’s negative points come largely from user-review summaries and competitor analyses, so they should be validated with interviews rather than treated as universal defects. In particular, interview:

- Technicians working in poor-connectivity areas.
- Facilities managers handling multiple buildings.
- Procurement teams managing spare parts and contractors.
- Compliance managers responsible for statutory inspections.
- Reliability engineers who currently export CMMS data to Excel or BI tools.
- Small organizations priced out by per-user licensing.

Ask them to demonstrate their last five maintenance jobs, not merely describe desired features. The strongest opportunity will be the step where they still use WhatsApp, paper, spreadsheets, phone calls, or repeated manual data entry.
